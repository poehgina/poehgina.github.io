Gina Pöhlmann

I am a technology governance and policy researcher studying whose perspectives shape decisions about AI and quantum technologies, and how the people affected by them can be involved earlier. Currently, I am a PhD candidate at the University of St. Gallen, where I work in the EU-funded Q-Shift consortium, and a Recognized Academic Visitor at the Responsible Innovation Institute, University of Oxford. I expect to complete my PhD in early 2027.

My research combines quantitative text analysis of policy, parliamentary and media documents with expert interviews and participatory, AI-assisted methods. I am interested in AI governance in which technology strengthens, rather than replaces, human knowledge and judgment. Before my PhD, I worked in insights, analytics and communications at WarnerMedia, Mercedes-Benz and Audi.

Research

Current Questions

Whose knowledge counts Debates on quantum technologies and AI are dominated by competition and security narratives, while the perspectives of the people affected receive little attention. How can AI governance draw on the practical knowledge of the many people who use and are affected by AI, rather than concentrating decisions in a few hands?
AI that widens human participation How can AI help hear from more people while complementing, not replacing, human engagement, and how should we assess it by what people achieve with AI rather than by what AI does alone?
Governing under uncertainty How can decisions about releasing and adapting AI models stay adjustable as evidence grows?
